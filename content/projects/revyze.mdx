## The motivation

Revyze began with a personal challenge. While helping my children prepare for their examinations, I purchased past-year examination papers and realised that there was an opportunity to make better use of the material beyond simply completing one paper after another.

I wanted to understand which questions appeared frequently, which topics were consistently examined, how marks were distributed and where students could focus their revision efforts more effectively.

Rather than relying entirely on conventional revision methods, I decided to explore how software engineering and data analysis could provide a more structured approach.

## The approach

I extracted most of the questions from the purchased examination papers, including their associated diagrams and illustrations, and transformed them into a structured question bank.

The objective was to make historical examination material searchable, categorisable and suitable for analysis across multiple examination years.

This created a foundation for exploring:

- Frequently recurring questions and question patterns.
- Topics that consistently appear across examination years.
- Distribution of marks across topics and question types.
- Questions and topics carrying higher marks.
- Similar questions presented in different formats.
- Areas requiring greater revision attention.
- Topic-based and question-based assessments.
- Student performance and knowledge gaps.

The intention is not to predict future examination questions, but to use historical examination patterns to support more informed and focused revision.

## Key engineering areas

- Document processing and question extraction.
- Extraction and preservation of examination diagrams.
- Structured question bank design.
- Educational content classification.
- Historical examination pattern analysis.
- Question frequency analysis.
- Marks distribution analysis.
- Digital assessment workflows.
- Learning progress tracking.
- Problem-driven product development.

## Engineering philosophy

The project explores how unstructured educational material can be transformed into structured, actionable information.

Rather than simply digitising examination papers, the aim is to unlock the value of the information contained within them and make revision more purposeful.
